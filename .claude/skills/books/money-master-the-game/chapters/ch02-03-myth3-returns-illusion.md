# Chapter 2.3: Myth 3: "Our Returns? What You See Is What You Get"

## Core Idea
The returns advertised by mutual funds (time-weighted) don't match what investors actually earn (dollar-weighted)—creating a 3% annual gap where funds advertise 6% but investors achieve 3%, compounded over decades this difference is massive.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Average Return Illusion**: Sequence of +50%, -50%, +50%, -50% averages to 0%, but actual result is -43.75%—mathematical impossibility of recovering from percentage losses
- **Time-Weighted vs Dollar-Weighted Returns**: Time-weighted = theoretical Day 1 lump sum (used for advertising); Dollar-weighted = real contributions/withdrawals throughout year (actual result)
- **3% Performance Gap**: Bogle's comparison shows investors lag funds by 3% annually—advertise 6%, investors get 3%
- **Lipstick on a Pig**: Charles Schwab ad exposing industry practice—"Tell clients it's red hot! Just don't mention the fundamentals—they stink"

## Key Concepts
- **Average Return Deception**: Dow Jones since 1930—"averages" 6.31% but "actual" 4.31%; $1K at 6.31% = $142K, at 4.31% = $30K (4.7x difference)
- **Volatility Drag**: Equal percentage gains and losses don't cancel—starting $100K, down 50% = $50K, up 50% = $75K (net -25%)
- **Mutual Fund Return Calculation**: Funds assume all money invested Day 1—doesn't account for ongoing contributions from paychecks
- **Performance Chasing Effect**: Investors contribute more when funds performing well (buying high), less when performing poorly (selling low)—worsens dollar-weighted returns
- **Moneychimp Solution**: Calculator at discount_rate_calculator.htm shows actual return on money over period—bypass advertised averages

## Mental Models
- **Profile Photos for Online Dating**: Average returns paint better portrait than reality—glossy marketing vs actual experience
- **Sleight of Pencil Magic**: Industry uses tricky calculation methods to publish returns "not actually earned by investors"
- **Theoretical Person Fallacy**: Reported returns based on someone who invested everything Day 1—most people contribute throughout year via paycheck deductions
- **Real World vs Advertising**: Dollar-weighted returns = what we actually keep; Time-weighted returns = fuel for full-page ads
- **Machete of Truth**: Hacking away lies gives clear path—no longer flying blind, separated from "sheeple"

## Anti-patterns
- **Believing Average Returns**: 0% average sounds like break-even, but can be -43.75% actual with volatility
- **Comparing Fund Returns to Personal Results**: Your experience will differ due to contribution timing—chasing performance makes it worse
- **Assuming Advertised = Achievable**: 6% advertised return typically becomes 3% after accounting for real-world behavior
- **Trusting Past Performance Numbers**: Even when reported accurately, they don't represent what you'll earn

## Key Takeaways
1. **3% Annual Gap**: Investors achieve 3% less than advertised returns—fund advertises 6%, investor gets closer to 3%
2. **Volatility Destroys Wealth**: +50%, -50%, +50%, -50% sequence averages 0% but loses 43.75%—can't recover from percentage losses
3. **Dow Jones Example**: 1930-present—"average" 6.31% vs "actual" 4.31%—$1K grows to $142K vs $30K (4.7x difference)
4. **Time-Weighted Used for Ads**: Funds calculate returns assuming Day 1 lump sum—doesn't reflect ongoing contributions
5. **Calculate Your Actual Return**: Use Moneychimp calculator—input starting amount, ending amount, time period to see true return
6. **Bogle's Crusade**: Vanguard founder wants investors to see dollar-weighted returns (including contributions/withdrawals)—industry resists
7. **Performance Chasing Worsens Gap**: Investors pile in when funds hot (buying high), abandon when cold (selling low)—exactly wrong behavior
