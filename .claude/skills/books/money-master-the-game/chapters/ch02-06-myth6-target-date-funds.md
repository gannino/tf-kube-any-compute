# Chapter 2.6: Myth 6: Target-Date Funds: "Just Set It and Forget It"

## Core Idea
Target-date funds (TDFs) are 401(k) default investments marketed as "set it and forget it"—but 41% of participants use them assuming safety and guarantees that don't exist. In 2008, many "2010" funds (2 years from retirement) lost 30-40% because managers chose high stock allocations.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Pay-to-Play Fees**: 90% of 401(k) plans require revenue sharing—mutual funds pay to get on menu, recoup costs through higher fees
- **Glide Path Deception**: Fund managers pick own schedule for decreasing stocks/increasing bonds—no uniform standard, each "slippery slope" differs
- **Two False Presumptions**: (1) Bonds are safe (Buffett says "warning label"), (2) Bonds move opposite stocks (80% correlation in 2008)
- **TDF Misconceptions Survey**: 57% think no 10-year losses, 30% think guaranteed return, 62% think date = retirement readiness
- **David Babbel's DOL Counterstudy**: Wharton professor's study showed TDFs inferior to stable value funds—industry paid $2M for favorable study

## Key Concepts
- **Fastest-Growing Segment**: TDFs largest growth area in mutual fund industry—trillions invested, 96% of large employers use as default
- **No Guaranteed Outcome**: TDFs are just asset allocation funds—not plans to reach goals, just become "less risky" over time
- **41% Participation**: By end of 2013, 41% of 401(k) participants in TDFs—auto-enrollment drives adoption
- **2006 Paved Way**: Federal law made TDFs default option—employers can't be held liable for using them
- **96% Large Employers**: Use TDFs as default investment choice—Fidelity research data
- **2010 Funds in 2008**: Many near-retirement funds had 60-80% stock allocations—lost 30-40% when market crashed
- **Gliding Path Variation**: Each fund manager picks own schedule—no standard, "slippery slope" not glide path

## Mental Models
- **Set It and Forget It**: Seductive marketing—pick date, never look again, trust us to handle it (dangerous illusion)
- **Just Asset Allocation**: You're buying fund that handles allocation—simple wrapper, not magic solution
- **Bonds Warning Label**: Warren Buffett's view—bonds not safe, fall when rates rise
- **Swimming Naked Until Tide Out**: 2008 revealed who was taking risks—near-retirement funds devastated
- **$2M Bought Approval**: Industry investment in favorable study—bought seal of approval from Labor Department

## Anti-patterns
- **"TDFs Are Safe"**: 2010 funds lost 30-40% in 2008—some near-retirement funds 80% stocks
- **"Date = Retirement Ready"**: 62% think target date means can retire—false, cruel misconception
- **"Set and Forget"**: Never reviewing dangerous—allocation can vary wildly between providers
- **"Default = Best Choice"**: Employers chose TDFs for liability protection, not employee benefit
- **"Guaranteed Return"**: 30% think TDFs guarantee returns—no guarantees whatsoever

## Key Takeaways
1. **Pay-to-Play Dominates**: 90% of 401(k) plans require revenue sharing—choices maximize vendor profits
2. **Three Jaw-Dropping Misconceptions**: 57% think no losses possible, 30% think guaranteed return, 62% think date = retirement
3. **Bonds Not Safe**: Buffett says warning label needed—fall when rates rise, correlated with stocks in bad times
4. **2008 Devastation**: 2010 TDFs lost 30-40%—some 80% stocks despite "conservative" label
5. **No Uniform Standard**: Each manager picks own glide path—wild variation in allocations
6. **Industry $2M Study**: Bought favorable DOL report—independent study showed flaws
7. **Auto-Enrollment Risk**: 96% large employers auto-enroll into TDFs—participants don't understand risks
8. **Vanguard Exception**: Vanguard's ultra-low-cost TDFs noted as exception to expensive, underperforming norm
