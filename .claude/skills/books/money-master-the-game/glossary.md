# MONEY Master the Game — Glossary

**401(k)** — Employer-sponsored retirement savings plan that allows workers to save and invest a portion of their paycheck before taxes are taken out. (Ch 2.5)

**Active Management** — Investment strategy where fund managers actively buy and sell securities in an attempt to outperform the market. (Ch 2.1, 6.2)

**All Seasons Strategy** — Ray Dalio's portfolio allocation designed to perform well in any economic environment by balancing assets across four economic seasons. (Ch 5.1, 6.6)

**Alpha** — Measure of investment performance relative to a benchmark index; positive alpha means outperformance. (Ch 6.6)

**Annuity** — Insurance product that provides guaranteed income payments for life or a specified period. (Ch 2.7, 4.2, 5.3)

**Asset Allocation** — The process of dividing investments among different asset classes (stocks, bonds, cash, etc.) to optimize risk-adjusted returns. (Ch 2.0, 4.1)

**Asset Classes** — Categories of investments with similar characteristics (stocks, bonds, real estate, commodities, cash equivalents). (Ch 4.1, 6.2)

**Asymmetric Risk/Reward** — Investment principle where potential upside significantly exceeds potential downside (e.g., 5:1 ratio). (Ch 6.0, 6.1, 6.5)

**AUM (Assets Under Management)** — Total market value of investments that a financial institution or fund manager manages. (Ch 6.2, 6.7)

**Bear Market** — Market condition where securities prices fall 20% or more from recent highs, typically accompanied by widespread pessimism. (Ch 6.5)

**Behavioral Economics** — Study of psychological factors that influence economic decisions and financial behavior. (Ch 1.3)

**Benchmark** — Standard against which performance of a security or investment manager is measured. (Ch 2.1)

**Beta** — Measure of volatility relative to the overall market; beta of 1 means moves with the market. (Ch 6.6)

**Blue Chip Stocks** — Shares of large, well-established, and financially sound companies with history of reliable operations. (Ch 6.8)

**Bond** — Debt investment where an investor loans money to an entity (corporate or governmental) for a defined period at a fixed interest rate. (Ch 4.1, 4.2)

**Bull Market** — Market condition where securities prices are rising or expected to rise, typically accompanied by investor optimism. (Ch 6.5)

**Capital Gains** — Profit from sale of an asset (stock, bond, real estate) that has increased in value. (Ch 2.8, 6.4)

**Capital Gains Tax** — Tax on profits from sale of investments or property. (Ch 2.8, 6.4)

**Case-Shiller Index** — Leading measure of US residential real estate prices, tracking changes in value of residential real estate. (Ch 4.2)

**Cash Drag** — Negative impact on returns when portfolio holds uninvested cash. (Ch 6.3)

**Collateral** — Asset pledged to secure a loan; can be seized if borrower defaults. (Ch 4.2)

**Compounding** — Process where investment earnings generate additional earnings over time; exponential growth of wealth. (Ch 1.3, 3.6)

**Compounding Frequency** — How often earned interest is added to principal (daily, monthly, annually). (Ch 5.3)

**Contribution** — Money added to an investment account (401k, IRA, etc.). (Ch 3.3)

**Convertible Bond** — Bond that can be converted into predetermined number of shares of issuing company's stock. (Ch 6.1)

**Correlation** — Statistical measure of how two securities move in relation to each other. (Ch 4.1, 5.1)

**Cost Drag** — Reduction in investment returns caused by fees, expenses, and transaction costs. (Ch 2.2, 6.3)

**Credit Default Swap (CDS)** — Financial derivative that allows investors to swap or offset credit risk. (Ch 6.2)

**Currency Swap** — Foreign exchange agreement between two parties to exchange principal and/or interest payments. (Ch 6.2)

**Debt-to-Equity Ratio** — Financial metric comparing total debt to shareholders' equity; indicates financial leverage. (Ch 6.8)

**Defensive Investing** — Strategy focused on protecting capital and minimizing losses rather than maximizing gains. (Ch 6.5)

**Diversification** — Risk management strategy that mixes various investments within portfolio to reduce exposure to any single asset. (Ch 4.1, 5.1)

**Dividend** — Distribution of portion of company's earnings to shareholders, usually quarterly. (Ch 6.3)

**Dividend Yield** — Annual dividend payment divided by stock's current price. (Ch 6.3)

**Dollar-Cost Averaging** — Investment strategy of investing fixed amount at regular intervals regardless of price. (Ch 4.4)

**Drawdown** — Decline from peak to trough in value of investment or portfolio during specific period. (Ch 6.5, 6.6)

**Dream Bucket** — Portion of portfolio allocated for investments with higher risk and higher potential rewards for fulfilling dreams. (Ch 4.3)

**Duration** — Measure of sensitivity of bond price to interest rate changes. (Ch 5.1)

**Economic Moat** — Competitive advantage that protects business profitability and market share. (Ch 6.4)

**Emerging Markets** — Economies of developing nations with potential for high growth but higher risk. (Ch 4.1, 6.2)

**ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund)** — Investment fund traded on stock exchanges, similar to stocks but holding assets like stocks, commodities, or bonds. (Ch 2.8)

**Expense Ratio** — Annual fee that funds charge shareholders, expressed as percentage of assets. (Ch 2.2, 6.2, 6.3)

**Fiduciary** — Person or organization legally required to act in best interest of another party. (Ch 2.4, 6.2)

**Fiduciary Duty** — Legal obligation to act in best interest of another party, putting client interests ahead of own. (Ch 2.4, 6.2)

**Financial Freedom** — State where investment income covers living expenses without need to work. (Ch 1.1)

**First Trust Deed** — Mortgage document that gives lender first claim to property if borrower defaults. (Ch 4.2)

**Fixed Annuity** — Annuity that provides guaranteed fixed payments for life or specified period. (Ch 5.3)

**Fixed Income** — Investments that provide return in form of fixed periodic payments and eventual return of principal at maturity. (Ch 4.1, 4.2)

**Free Lunch Principle** — Harry Markowitz's insight that diversification is a "free lunch"—reduces risk without sacrificing returns. (Ch 6.2)

**Fundamental Analysis** — Method of evaluating security by examining related economic, financial, and other qualitative/quantitative factors. (Ch 6.10)

**Growth Bucket** — Portion of portfolio allocated to higher-risk investments with potential for higher returns. (Ch 4.2)

**Hedge** — Investment made to reduce risk of adverse price movements in another asset. (Ch 5.1)

**Hedge Fund** — Aggressively managed portfolio that uses advanced investment strategies to generate high returns. (Ch 6.0, 6.2)

**High-Frequency Trading** — Automated trading platform that uses powerful computers to transact large number of orders at extremely high speeds. (Ch 6.3)

**Holding Period** — Length of time an investment is held before being sold. (Ch 6.11)

**Index Fund** — Mutual fund or ETF designed to track components of market index (S&P 500, etc.) with low fees. (Ch 2.1, 6.2, 6.3)

**Inflation** — Rate at which general level of prices for goods and services rises, eroding purchasing power. (Ch 3.1, 4.1, 5.1)

**Inflation-Protected Securities** — Bonds (like TIPS) that provide protection against inflation by adjusting principal value. (Ch 4.1, 5.1)

**Interest Rate Risk** — Potential for investment losses due to changes in interest rates. (Ch 5.1)

**Investment Advisory Fee** — Fee charged for professional investment advice and portfolio management. (Ch 2.2)

**IRA (Individual Retirement Account)** — Tax-advantaged retirement savings account. (Ch 2.5, 5.3)

**Junk Bond** — High-yield, high-risk corporate bond with lower credit rating. (Ch 6.1)

**Leverage** — Use of borrowed money to increase potential return of investment; also amplifies risk. (Ch 4.3, 6.9)

**Liability** — Financial debt or obligation of individual or company. (Ch 3.1)

**Limiting Beliefs** — Negative mental patterns or assumptions that constrain behavior and potential. (Ch 1.4)

**Liquidity** — Ease with which asset can be converted into cash without significantly affecting its price. (Ch 4.1, 4.2)

**Load** — Sales charge commission paid when buying (front-end load) or selling (back-end load) mutual fund shares. (Ch 2.2)

**Long-Term Capital Gains Tax** — Tax on profits from sale of assets held more than one year. (Ch 2.8, 6.4)

**Market Capitalization** — Total dollar market value of company's outstanding shares of stock. (Ch 6.4)

**Market Timing** — Attempting to predict future price movements to buy low and sell high. (Ch 2.1, 4.4, 6.3)

**Master Fee (Wrap Fee)** — Comprehensive fee covering investment management and sometimes other services. (Ch 2.2)

**Maturity Date** — Date on which principal of bond, note, or other debt instrument becomes due and payable. (Ch 4.2)

**Modern Portfolio Theory** — Mathematical framework for assembling portfolio of assets that maximizes expected return for given level of risk. (Ch 6.2)

**Money Market Fund** — Mutual fund that invests in high-quality, short-term debt securities. (Ch 4.1, 4.2)

**Municipal Bond** — Debt security issued by state, municipality, or county to finance capital expenditures. (Ch 4.1, 4.2)

**Mutual Fund** — Investment vehicle funded by shareholders that trades in diversified holdings. (Ch 2.1, 6.2, 6.3)

**Net Worth** — Total assets minus total liabilities; measure of wealth. (Ch 3.1, 3.2)

**Nominal Return** — Return on investment without adjustment for inflation. (Ch 3.1)

**Opportunity Cost** — Potential benefit lost when choosing one alternative over another. (Ch 3.3)

**Passive Management** — Investment strategy that tracks market index rather than actively selecting securities. (Ch 2.1, 6.3)

**Portfolio** — Collection of investments held by individual or institution. (Ch 4.1)

**Principal** — Original amount of money invested or borrowed, excluding interest or returns. (Ch 4.2, 5.3)

**Principal Protection** — Guarantee that initial investment will not be lost. (Ch 5.3)

**Prospectus** — Legal document that describes investment and offering facts to potential investors. (Ch 2.7)

**Pure Alpha** — Ray Dalio's strategy of generating returns uncorrelated with market movements. (Ch 6.6)

**Ratchet Feature** | Annuity provision that locks in investment gains as lifetime income base. (Ch 5.3)

**Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT)** | Company that owns or finances income-producing real estate. (Ch 4.2, 6.2)

**Real Return** | Return on investment adjusted for inflation; purchasing power gained. (Ch 3.1)

**Rebalancing** | Process of realigning portfolio holdings to target asset allocation percentages. (Ch 4.1, 4.4)

**Required Minimum Distribution (RMD)** | Minimum amount that must be withdrawn from tax-advantaged retirement accounts annually. (Ch 5.4)

**Retention Bonus** | Compensation incentive to keep employee with company for specified period. (Ch 6.7)

**Return of Capital** | Payment that returns investor's original investment, not taxable as income. (Ch 5.4)

**Risk** | Potential for loss or underperformance of investment. (Ch 4.1, 4.2)

**Risk/Growth Bucket** | Portion of portfolio allocated to investments with higher risk and higher potential returns. (Ch 4.2)

**Risk Tolerance** | Degree of variability in investment returns that investor is willing to withstand. (Ch 4.1)

**Roth IRA** | Individual retirement account where contributions are made after-tax, but qualified withdrawals are tax-free. (Ch 2.5, 5.3)

**Security Bucket** | Portion of portfolio allocated to safe, stable investments that preserve capital. (Ch 4.2)

**Shareholder** | Individual or institution that legally owns shares of stock in corporation. (Ch 6.3)

**Sharpe Ratio** | Measure of risk-adjusted return; higher ratio indicates better performance for risk taken. (Ch 6.6)

**Short Selling** | Selling borrowed securities with intention of repurchasing them later at lower price. (Ch 6.1)

**Small-Cap Stocks** | Shares of companies with relatively small market capitalization (typically $300M-$2B). (Ch 6.8)

**Social Security** | US federal government social insurance program that provides benefits to retired and disabled individuals. (Ch 2.5, 5.4)

**Standard Deviation** | Statistical measure of market volatility or investment risk. (Ch 6.6)

**Stock** | Type of security that signifies ownership in corporation and represents claim on part of corporation's assets and earnings. (Ch 4.2, 6.4)

**Strategic Asset Allocation** | Portfolio strategy that sets target allocations for various asset classes and periodically rebalances. (Ch 4.1)

**Structured Note** | Debt security whose payoff is linked to performance of underlying asset or index. (Ch 4.2, 5.5)

**Survivorship Bias** | Logical error of concentrating on people or things that survived some process and overlooking those that didn't. (Ch 2.1, 6.2)

**Target-Date Fund** | Mutual fund that automatically adjusts asset allocation mix as investor approaches target retirement date. (Ch 2.6)

**Tax Alpha** | Investment returns generated through tax-efficient strategies. (Ch 6.11)

**Tax Deferral** | Delaying payment of taxes on investment income until future date. (Ch 2.5, 5.3)

**Tax Efficiency** | Measure of how much investment returns are reduced by taxes. (Ch 2.8, 6.2, 6.4)

**Tax-Equivalent Yield** | Pretax yield that taxable bond must offer to match after-tax yield of tax-free municipal bond. (Ch 4.1)

**Technical Analysis** | Forecasting direction of prices through study of past market data, primarily price and volume. (Ch 6.5)

**Term Life Insurance** | Life insurance that provides coverage for specified period; pays benefit only if death occurs during term. (Ch 4.2)

**TIPS (Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities)** | US Treasury bond indexed to inflation; protects purchasing power. (Ch 4.1, 5.1)

**Treasury Bond** | US government debt security with fixed interest rate and maturity. (Ch 4.1, 5.1)

**Trend Following** | Investment strategy based on analyzing price trends; buying assets that are rising, selling those falling. (Ch 6.5)

**Turnover Ratio** | Measure of fund's trading activity; percentage of portfolio holdings that have been replaced over period. (Ch 2.2)

**Uncorrelated Assets** | Investments whose price movements are not related; provide diversification benefits. (Ch 5.1)

**Variable Annuity** | Annuity contract where returns vary based on performance of underlying investments. (Ch 2.7, 5.3)

**Volatility** | Degree of variation of trading price series over time; measure of risk. (Ch 4.1, 6.6)

**Whole Life Insurance** | Life insurance with savings component that builds cash value over time. (Ch 5.5)

**Withdrawal Rate** | Percentage of portfolio withdrawn annually during retirement. (Ch 5.4)

**Yield** | Income return on investment (interest or dividends) expressed as percentage of investment price. (Ch 6.3)

**Yield Curve** | Graph showing relationship between interest rates and equal maturity dates for similar debt securities. (Ch 5.1)

**Zero-Coupon Bond** | Bond that doesn't pay periodic interest but is sold at discount and redeemed at face value. (Ch 5.1)
