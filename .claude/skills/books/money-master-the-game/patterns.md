# MONEY Master the Game — Patterns & Techniques

## The 7 Simple Steps to Financial Freedom

**When to use**: Overall framework for achieving financial independence
**How**: Follow each step sequentially: (1) Commit to savings, (2) Know the rules (myths), (3) Dream price & plan, (4) Asset allocation, (5) Lifetime income plan, (6) Learn from masters, (7) Enjoy & share
**Trade-offs**: Requires long-term discipline; immediate sacrifices for future freedom
**Source**: Ch 1.2, 1.3, 7.1

---

## All Seasons Strategy

**When to use**: Building portfolio that performs in any economic environment
**How**: Allocate across four economic seasons using specific percentages: Stocks (30%), Long-term Treasury Bonds (40%), Intermediate-term Treasury Bonds (15%), Gold (7.5%), Commodities (7.5%)
**Trade-offs**: Lower upside during pure bull markets but protects in downturns; requires understanding of economic seasons
**Source**: Ch 5.1, 6.6

---

## Three-Bucket System

**When to use**: Organizing portfolio for different goals and risk levels
**How**: (1) Security Bucket - safe investments for lifetime income, (2) Risk/Growth Bucket - investments for growth, (3) Dream Bucket - money for enjoying life
**Trade-offs**: Requires balancing between buckets; Security Bucket may feel too conservative for young investors
**Source**: Ch 4.1-4.3

---

## Money Machine (Automatic Investing)

**When to use**: Creating consistent, automated savings and investment system
**How**: Set up automatic deductions from paycheck to investment accounts; increase by 1% every 6 months or with each raise
**Trade-offs**: Requires initial setup and discipline; reduces discretionary income now
**Source**: Ch 1.3, 3.3

---

## 1-3-5 Formula (Speed Up Your Wealth)

**When to use**: Accelerating progress toward financial freedom
**How**: (1) Save More & Invest, (2) Earn More & Invest, (3) Reduce Fees & Taxes, (4) Get Better Returns, (5) Change Lifestyle
**Trade-offs**: Lifestyle changes require sacrifice; earning more may require time/effort
**Source**: Ch 3.3-3.7

---

## Asymmetric Risk/Reward

**When to use**: Making investment decisions with favorable risk profiles
**How**: Risk 1 to make 5; allows 80% failure rate and still break even; look for opportunities where upside >> downside
**Trade-offs**: Requires skill and patience to find these opportunities; not all investments offer this profile
**Source**: Ch 6.0, 6.1, 6.5

---

## 200-Day Moving Average Strategy

**When to use**: Determining market trends for buy/sell decisions
**How**: Track 200-day moving average; buy when price above, sell when drops below
**Trade-offs**: Lagging indicator; may miss some gains or sell too early; works better with volatile assets
**Source**: Ch 6.5

---

## Defense First Principle

**When to use**: All investment decisions; prioritizing capital preservation
**How**: Defense is 10x more important than offense; focus first on "don't lose," then on growth
**Trade-offs**: May miss some opportunities; requires discipline to stay defensive during bull markets
**Source**: Ch 6.0, 6.5

---

## Index Fund Investing

**When to use**: Core portfolio holdings for most investors
**How**: Buy low-cost index funds tracking broad markets (Vanguard, etc.); hold long-term; minimize trading
**Trade-offs**: Won't beat market (will match market); requires patience; no "bragging rights" from stock picking
**Source**: Ch 2.1, 6.2, 6.3

---

## Fee Reduction Strategy

**When to use**: Improving investment returns immediately
**How**: Audit all fees (expense ratios, advisory fees, transaction costs); aim for <0.5% total; use index funds and fiduciary advisors
**Trade-offs**: May need to change providers or investment vehicles; some "premium" services may be unavailable
**Source**: Ch 2.2, 2.8

---

## Tax-Efficient Investing

**When to use**: Maximizing after-tax returns
**How**: Prioritize tax-advantaged accounts (401k, IRA); use tax-efficient funds (index); hold long-term to qualify for lower rates
**Trade-offs**: Limits investment choices; contributions have annual limits; early withdrawal penalties
**Source**: Ch 2.8, 6.4

---

## Rebalancing Strategy

**When to use**: Maintaining target asset allocation
**How**: Review portfolio quarterly or annually; sell assets that grew above target percentage; buy underweighted assets
**Trade-offs**: May trigger taxes in taxable accounts; requires discipline to sell winners and buy laggers
**Source**: Ch 4.1, 4.4

---

## Dollar-Cost Averaging

**When to use**: Regular investing regardless of market conditions
**How**: Invest fixed amount at regular intervals (monthly); ignore market fluctuations
**Trade-offs**: Won't maximize returns in consistently rising markets; requires discipline during downturns
**Source**: Ch 4.4

---

## Core-Satellite Approach

**When to use**: Balancing passive and active investing
**How**: Core (70-80%) in low-cost index funds; Satellite (20-30%) in active investments or specific opportunities
**Trade-offs**: More complex than pure index approach; satellite portion requires more oversight
**Source**: Ch 6.2, 6.3

---

## Yale Model (Endowment Model)

**When to use**: Diversified portfolio across multiple asset classes
**How**: Allocate across 5-6 roughly equal parts: US stocks, foreign developed/emerging stocks, bonds, TIPS, REITs; 70% equity, 30% fixed income
**Trade-offs**: Requires periodic rebalancing; some asset classes may be less accessible to individual investors
**Source**: Ch 4.1, 6.2

---

## 5-to-1 Rule

**When to use**: Evaluating any investment opportunity
**How**: Calculate potential upside vs. potential downside; only invest if upside is at least 5x the risk
**Trade-offs**: Limits investment opportunities; requires accurate risk assessment
**Source**: Ch 6.0, 6.5

---

## Trend Following

**When to use**: Making investment entry/exit decisions
**How**: Align with predominant market trend; never be contrarian; use technical indicators (200-day MA)
**Trade-offs**: Misses turning points; doesn't predict tops/bottoms; can be whipsawed in choppy markets
**Source**: Ch 6.5

---

## Concentrated Value Investing

**When to use**: High-conviction investments with asymmetric upside
**How**: Focus heavily on few undervalued opportunities; thorough research; activist approach when needed
**Trade-offs**: High risk through lack of diversification; requires deep expertise; requires patient capital
**Source**: Ch 6.1

---

## Buy and Hold

**When to use**: Long-term investing in quality companies
**How**: Buy great companies and never sell; hold for decades; let compounding work
**Trade-offs**: Requires patience; ties up capital; may hold through significant downturns
**Source**: Ch 6.4, 6.12

---

## Home Bias Hedge

**When to use**: Balancing domestic vs. international investments
**How**: Never allocate 100% to home country; diversify globally; learn from Japan's 1990s experience
**Trade-offs**: Currency risk; political risk in foreign markets; may require specialized investment vehicles
**Source**: Ch 6.2

---

## Inflation Protection Strategy

**When to use**: Protecting purchasing power in retirement
**How**: Hold inflation-protected securities (TIPS); invest in real assets (real estate, commodities); avoid excessive cash
**Trade-offs**: TIPS have lower yields; real assets can be volatile; requires ongoing monitoring
**Source**: Ch 4.1, 5.1

---

## Lifetime Income Planning

**When to use**: Creating retirement cash flow that lasts as long as you live
**How**: Combine Social Security, pensions, annuities, and systematic withdrawals; plan for longevity (100+ years)
**Trade-offs**: Requires locking up capital; annuities reduce legacy; conservative assumptions mean less current spending
**Source**: Ch 5.3-5.4

---

## 4% Rule (Safe Withdrawal Rate)

**When to use**: Planning retirement withdrawals
**How**: Withdraw 4% of portfolio annually, adjusted for inflation; based on historical market performance
**Trade-offs**: May be too aggressive in low-return environments; doesn't guarantee success; requires flexibility in spending
**Source**: Ch 5.4

---

## Ratchet Strategy

**When to use**: Locking in investment gains while maintaining lifetime income
**How**: Use annuities with ratchet features that lock in investment gains as lifetime income base
**Trade-offs**: Complex products; fees may be higher; requires understanding of contract terms
**Source**: Ch 5.3

---

## Philanthropic Leverage

**When to use**: Maximizing impact of charitable giving
**How**: Use donor-advised funds, private foundations, or Robin Hood model where board covers all costs
**Trade-offs**: Administrative overhead; legal requirements; may require minimum contribution levels
**Source**: Ch 5.5, 6.5

---

## PPLI (Private Placement Life Insurance)

**When to use**: Ultra-wealthy tax efficiency strategy
**How**: Wrap investments in life insurance policy; tax-deferred growth; tax-free loans; creditor protection
**Trade-offs**: High minimums ($1M-$5M+); complex structure; not suitable for most investors
**Source**: Ch 5.5

---

## Tax-Loss Harvesting

**When to use**: Reducing taxable investment gains
**How**: Sell investments at loss to offset gains; reinvest in similar but not identical securities; carry forward losses
**Trade-offs**: Transaction costs; wash sale rules; requires ongoing management
**Source**: Ch 2.8, 6.11

---

## Principal-Protected Investing

**When to use**: Conservative investors or near-retirement
**How**: Use structured notes, annuities, CDs, or Treasury bonds; guarantee return of principal; participate in some upside
**Trade-offs**: Caps upside potential; issuer credit risk; may underperform during bull markets
**Source**: Ch 4.2, 5.3

---

## Reversion to Mean

**When to use**: Avoiding chasing past performance
**How**: Understand that past winners often become future losers; don't chase 4-5 star funds; stick to systematic approach
**Trade-offs**: Requires discipline; means not chasing "hot" investments
**Source**: Ch 2.1, 6.2

---

## Market Cycle Awareness

**When to use**: Understanding current investment environment
**How**: Recognize that bull and bear markets change quickly; fortunes made and lost rapidly; prepare for all scenarios
**Trade-offs**: Can lead to overtrading if misapplied; timing the market is difficult
**Source**: Ch 6.5

---

## Cash as Ammo Strategy

**When to use**: Waiting for attractive investment opportunities
**How**: Hold cash when no compelling value; maintain firepower for opportunities; "cash is not worthless when no value exists"
**Trade-offs**: Opportunity cost of holding cash; requires patience and discipline
**Source**: Ch 6.5

---

## Contrarian Warning

**When to use**: Being aware of when contrarian investing is appropriate
**How**: 40% of people who would sell rising chart should never invest their own money; align with trend, don't fight it
**Trade-offs**: Misses some opportunities; requires understanding of market psychology
**Source**: Ch 6.5

---

## Turtle Wins Race Principle

**When to use**: Long-term investing perspective
**How**: Stay in game as long as possible through diversification and discipline; slow and steady beats fast and reckless
**Trade-offs**: Not exciting; requires patience; means not chasing hot tips
**Source**: Ch 6.5

---

## Three Decisions Framework

**When to use**: Making any significant life or financial decision
**How**: (1) What are you going to focus on? (2) What does it mean? (3) What are you going to do?
**Trade-offs**: Requires conscious decision-making; may feel artificial initially
**Source**: Ch 7.3

---

## Limiting Belief Identification

**When to use**: Overcoming mental obstacles to financial success
**How**: Identify beliefs about money that hold you back; question their validity; replace with empowering beliefs
**Trade-offs**: Requires introspection; may be emotionally difficult; ongoing practice required
**Source**: Ch 1.4

---

## Gratitude Practice

**When to use**: Maintaining perspective and happiness regardless of financial status
**How**: Regular practice of gratitude; focus on what you have, not what you lack; share with others
**Trade-offs**: May feel counterintuitive during financial stress; requires discipline
**Source**: Ch 7.2, 7.3

---

## Success Without Fulfillment

**When to use**: Evaluating life priorities and happiness
**How**: Recognize that achievement alone doesn't create happiness; focus on contribution and meaning
**Trade-offs**: May require reevaluating life goals; can be uncomfortable
**Source**: Ch 7.2

---

## Secret to Living is Giving

**When to use**: Creating fulfilling life and legacy
**How**: Practice generosity; share knowledge and resources; focus on contribution
**Trade-offs**: Reduces personal accumulation; may feel counterintuitive to wealth-building mindset
**Source**: Ch 7.2, 7.3

---

## Exponential Growth Mindset

**When to use**: Understanding technology and future opportunities
**How**: Recognize exponential trends in technology; anticipate disruption; position for future growth
**Trade-offs**: May overestimate near-term change; requires staying informed
**Source**: Ch 7.1
