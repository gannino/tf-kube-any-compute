# Chapter 6: The Core Four — Four Principles That Can Help Guide Every Investment Decision You Make

## Core Idea
The world's most successful investors share four fundamental decision-making principles: (1) Don't Lose, (2) Asymmetric Risk/Reward, (3) Tax Efficiency, and (4) Diversification. Mastering psychology (80% of success) is as important as mechanics (20%). These principles protect against losses, maximize upside opportunities, minimize tax drag, and reduce risk through intelligent diversification.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Core Principle 1: Don't Lose**: Avoiding losses is more important than chasing gains. A 50% loss requires a 100% gain just to break even. Losses compound destructively. "Rule #1: Never lose money. Rule #2: Never forget rule #1" (Warren Buffett).
- **100% Recovery Problem**: If you lose 50%, you need a 100% gain to break even. Lose 75% and you need 300% gain. This mathematical reality makes loss avoidance critical.
- **Core Principle 2: Asymmetric Risk/Reward**: Risk $1 to make $5 (Paul Tudor Jones' 5-to-1 rule). Seek disproportionate upside for limited downside. "Heads I win, tails I don't lose much."
- **Paul Tudor Jones' Five Questions**: Before any trade: (1) What's the hard trade? (2) What's asymmetric risk/reward? (3) Is it 5-to-1 or 3-to-1? (4) What's the entry point? (5) Where are the stops?
- **Core Principle 3: Tax Efficiency**: Focus on after-tax returns, not pre-tax. Use tax-advantaged accounts (401k, IRA, Roth IRA, HSA, 529 plans). "It's not what you earn, it's what you keep."
- **Asset Location Strategy**: Place tax-inefficient assets (bonds, REITs, high-dividend stocks) in tax-advantaged accounts. Place tax-efficient assets (index funds, ETFs) in taxable accounts.
- **Core Principle 4: Diversification — Four Levels**: (1) Across asset classes (stocks, bonds, real estate, gold, commodities), (2) Within asset classes (don't bet on one stock), (3) Across markets, countries, and currencies, (4) Across time (dollar-cost averaging).
- **80% Psychology, 20% Mechanics**: Success in investing is 80% psychology and 20% mechanics. Master your mind or it will master you. Behavioral biases cause costly mistakes.

## Key Concepts
- **Loss Aversion**: Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky's research shows financial losses cause twice as much pain as pleasure from equivalent gains. This makes investors act irrationally to avoid losses.
- **Negativity Bias**: Humans recall negative experiences more vividly than positive ones (evolutionary survival mechanism). In investing, this causes fear-based selling during downturns.
- **Confirmation Bias**: Seeking out information that confirms existing beliefs while avoiding contradictory evidence. Causes investors to fall in love with investments and ignore warning signs.
- **Recency Bias**: Projecting recent trends into the future indefinitely. "Investors project out into the future what they have most recently been seeing" (Warren Buffett).
- **Endowment Effect**: Placing greater value on what you already own regardless of objective value. Makes it harder to admit mistakes and pivot.
- **Hard Trade**: The investment that feels most uncomfortable or counterintuitive. Paul Tudor Jones seeks trades where others are fearful.
- **Tax Drag**: Taxes are often the largest expense in life. Active funds generate unnecessary tax bills through constant trading (short-term gains taxed at ordinary income rates).
- **Asset Allocation**: Investment success largely depends on how much you allocate to different asset classes, not which specific investments you pick.

## Mental Models
- **"First, Do No Harm"**: Medicine's fundamental principle applies equally to investing. Avoiding losses is more powerful than chasing gains because losses compound destructively.
- **Heads I Win, Tails I Don't Lose Much**: Asymmetric risk/reward means structuring investments where upside is substantial but downside is limited.
- **The House Always Wins**: Casinos collect tolls on every transaction. In investing, minimize the "tolls" you pay (fees, taxes, commissions) through tax efficiency and low costs.
- **GPS Navigation**: Treat your investment plan like GPS — if you make a wrong turn, it recalculates. Don't abandon the journey; adjust the route.
- **Seasonal Investor**: Understanding market seasons (corrections, bear markets, bull markets) removes fear. Winter always ends; spring always follows.
- **Survival Software**: The human brain is designed for survival, not happiness. It constantly scans for threats. Override this through awareness and systematic decision-making.

## Anti-patterns
- **"I Need to Find the Next Amazon"**: False — even pros can't consistently pick winners. Diversification protects against being wrong.
- **"High Fees Mean High Quality"**: False — high-fee funds underperform low-cost index funds 96% of the time. You're paying more for less.
- **"Tax-Deferred Means Tax-Free"**: False — you'll pay taxes eventually. Focus on after-tax returns and asset location.
- **"I Can Time the Market"**: False — must be right 69-91% of the time to succeed. Missing best days devastates returns.
- **"My Advisor Will Protect Me from Crashes"**: False — most advisors are brokers who profit from your fear. Prepare yourself in advance.
- **"Diversification Dilutes Returns"**: False — diversification increases risk-adjusted returns. You get the same returns with less volatility.
- **"I'll Handle Taxes Later"**: False — taxes compound like investment returns. Pay attention to tax efficiency from day one.

## Key Takeaways
1. **Don't Lose**: A 50% loss requires 100% gain to break even. Loss avoidance is more important than gain chasing.
2. **Asymmetric Risk/Reward**: Seek 5-to-1 opportunities where you risk $1 to make $5. Ask Paul Tudor Jones' five questions before investing.
3. **Tax Efficiency**: Focus on after-tax returns, not pre-tax. Use asset location to place tax-inefficient assets in tax-advantaged accounts.
4. **Four-Level Diversification**: Across asset classes, within asset classes, across countries/currencies, and across time through dollar-cost averaging.
5. **80% Psychology**: Success is mostly mastering your emotions and biases. Mechanics matter less than mindset.
6. **Confirmation Bias**: Actively seek qualified opinions that differ from your own. Warren Buffett consults Charlie Munger to challenge his thinking.
7. **Recency Bias**: Recent events feel like permanent trends. The market always looks forward, not backward.
8. **Negativity Bias & Loss Aversion**: Losses hurt twice as much as gains feel good. Prepare in advance for market turmoil so fear doesn't derail your plan.
9. **Preparation Is Key**: Write down why you own each asset. Have an advisor who can talk you through fears. Know your plan in advance.
10. **Master Your Mind**: You can't eliminate biases, but you can guard against them through systematic solutions and awareness.
11. **Investing Is a Game of Inches**: Small behavioral improvements (2-3% annually) compound to millions over decades through the power of compounding.
12. **No Perfect Score**: "This isn't about getting a perfect score. Even small improvements in our behavior can deliver enormous rewards" (Guy Spier).
