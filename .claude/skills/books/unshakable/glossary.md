# Unshakeable Glossary

**401(k) Dark Pool** — $6 trillion in retirement savings where providers extract excessive fees through 17+ hidden charge categories. Average worker loses $155K-$277K to fees over career lifetime. (Ch 4)

**Active Management** — Investment strategy where fund managers try to beat the market through stock picking and market timing. 96% of actively managed funds fail to beat the market over 15 years. (Ch 3)

**All-Weather Portfolio** — Ray Dalio's asset allocation model designed for stability in any economic environment: 30% stocks, 40% long-term bonds, 15% intermediate-term bonds, 7.5% gold, 7.5% commodities. (Ch 7)

**Amygdala Hijack** — Part of brain that acts as biological alarm system, flooding body with fear signals when losing money. Even minor market corrections trigger this survival reflex. (Ch 6)

**Asymmetric Risk/Reward** — Core Principle 2 of the Core Four. Risk $1 to make $5 (Paul Tudor Jones' five-to-one rule). "Heads I win, tails I don't lose much." (Ch 6)

**Asset Allocation** — Investment success largely depends on how much you allocate to different asset classes (stocks, bonds, real estate, gold, cash), not which specific investments you pick. Should be customized to your goals, not just based on age or risk tolerance. (Ch 6, 7)

**Asset Location Strategy** — Placing tax-inefficient assets (bonds, REITs, high-dividend stocks) in tax-advantaged accounts and tax-efficient assets (index funds, ETFs) in taxable accounts to maximize after-tax returns. (Ch 6)

**Beautiful State** — Emotional state of love, joy, gratitude, awe, playfulness, ease, creativity, drive, caring, growth, curiosity, appreciation. In this state, you know what to do and the best of you comes out. (Ch 8)

**Bear Market** — Market decline of 20% or more. 14 bear markets in US over last 70 years. Average duration ~1 year. Every US bear market has been followed by a bull market without exception. (Ch 2)

**Best Interest Contract Exemption (BICE)** — Document brokers may ask you to sign so they can "continue offering a full menu of options." Actually lets brokers keep selling profitable products with big commissions. Don't sign it. (Ch 5)

**Broker-Dealer Affiliation** — Look for "Securities offered through [company], member FINRA and SIPC" on business cards/websites. If you see it, they can act as a broker—run! (Ch 5)

**Broker vs. Fiduciary** — Brokers sell products for commissions and face no obligation to put clients first. Fiduciaries are legally required to act in clients' best interests. Most "financial advisors" are actually brokers. (Ch 5)

**Buckets Strategy (Three Buckets)** —
- Bucket 1: Security bucket (cash, bonds) for 0-5 years—protects during downturns
- Bucket 2: Growth bucket (stocks, real estate) for 5+ years—fuels long-term wealth
- Bucket 3: Dream bucket (enjoyment, legacy) for life goals
(Ch 7)

**Cash Drag** — Active funds hold cash for opportunities/redemptions. Cash earns no return, underperforming stocks over time. Forbes estimated 0.83% annual cost for actively managed funds. (Ch 3)

**Confirmation Bias** — Seeking out information that confirms existing beliefs while avoiding contradictory evidence. Causes investors to fall in love with investments and ignore warning signs. (Ch 6)

**Core Four Principles** — Four fundamental decision-making principles shared by 50+ financial masters:
1. Don't Lose (avoid losses, 100% gain needed to recover from 50% loss)
2. Asymmetric Risk/Reward (5-to-1 rule)
3. Tax Efficiency (after-tax returns focus)
4. Diversification (four levels)
(Ch 6)

**Correction** — Average market decline of 13.4%, occurs every 1.9 years. Brief and routine. (Ch 2)

**Diversification (Four Levels)** — Core Principle 4 of the Core Four:
1. Across asset classes (stocks, bonds, real estate, gold, commodities)
2. Within asset classes (don't bet on one stock)
3. Across markets, countries, and currencies
4. Across time (dollar-cost averaging)
(Ch 6)

**Don't Lose** — Core Principle 1 of the Core Four. Avoiding losses is more important than chasing gains. A 50% loss requires 100% gain to break even. "Rule #1: Never lose money. Rule #2: Never forget rule #1" (Warren Buffett). (Ch 6)

**Endowment Effect** — Placing greater value on what you already own regardless of objective value. Makes it harder to admit mistakes and pivot. (Ch 6)

**Expense Ratio** — Annual fee charged by mutual funds, typically 1-1.5% for stock funds. Covers advisory fee, administrative costs, office expenses. Only the beginning of fees—doesn't include transaction costs, cash drag, or tax costs. (Ch 3)

**Family Office Model** — Billionaires have in-house teams for comprehensive advice (investing, insurance, tax, estate planning). Peter Mallouk's Creative Planning offers this "family office for all" approach to clients with $100K+ assets. (Ch 5)

**Fiduciary** — Advisor who is legally required to act in clients' best interests. Tiny minority of financial advisors. Ask: "Are you a Registered Investment Advisor?" If no, they're a broker—run! (Ch 5)

**Fiduciary Liability** — Business owners are "plan sponsors" with legal obligation to act as fiduciaries for 401(k) plans. 75% of plans examined by DOL in 2014 were illegal. Average fine: $600K. (Ch 4)

**Financial Wealth vs. Real Wealth** — Financial wealth alone doesn't guarantee happiness. Real wealth is emotional, psychological, and spiritual. You can be financially rich AND emotionally rich—that's the ultimate prize. (Ch 9)

**Focus Creates Feelings** — "Whatever we focus on, we feel—regardless of what actually happened." Your focus creates your feelings, and your feelings create your experience. Reality doesn't matter—your perception does. (Ch 8)

**Front-End Load Abuse** — Some 401(k) providers take 5.75% of every deposit. Add 2% annual fees = 7.75% down before you start. "That's not a hole in your boat. That's the whole back of the boat ripped off." (Ch 4)

**Gratitude Meditation (Two-Minute Alignment)** — Simple technique to shift from suffering to beautiful state. Place hands on heart, breathe deeply, feel grateful for your heart, recall three grateful experiences, feel gratitude for present moment. Synchronizes heart and brain rhythms. (Ch 8)

**Grey Area Exploitation** — Some advisors claim to be fiduciaries but exploit legal grey areas to sell products that benefit themselves. "Unscrupulous salespeople who profited by misrepresenting themselves." (Ch 5)

**Hidden Fees (17 Categories)** — Beyond mutual fund expense ratios, 401(k) plans charge 17+ additional fees: investment expenses, communication expenses, bookkeeping expenses, administrative expenses, trustee expenses, legal expenses, transactional expenses, stewardship expenses, plus vague terms like "net asset charge," "asset-management charge," "contract asset charge," "AMC charge," "CAC charge," and "required revenue." (Ch 4)

**Index Fund** — Passive investment that tracks a market index (like S&P 500). Minimal trading → low transaction costs, no cash drag → fully invested, no manager salaries → lower fees, tax efficiency → long-term capital gains, protected from human error. Outperforms 96% of active funds over 15 years. (Ch 3)

**Loss Aversion** — Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky's research: financial losses cause twice as much pain as pleasure from equivalent gains. Makes investors act irrationally to avoid losses. (Ch 6, 7)

**Market Looks Forward** — Stock market prices where economy is headed, not where it is. Consumer confidence lows often precede strong returns (1974 +37%, 1980 +32%, 1990 +30%, 2008 +60%, 2011 +15%). (Ch 2)

**Mastering Your Mind** — Your biggest enemy is your own mind. The brain is wired for survival (two-million-year-old organ), not happiness. It constantly scans for threats and generates fear-based decisions. Solution: understand destructive patterns, choose your state deliberately, practice daily techniques. (Ch 8)

**Missing Best Days Cost** — S&P 500 returned 8.2% annually (1996-2015). Missing top 10 days → 4.5% returns. Missing top 20 days → 2.1% returns. Missing top 30 days → 0% returns. 6 of 10 best days occurred within 2 weeks of 10 worst days. (Ch 2)

**Mistakes to Avoid** — Six psychological mistakes that destroy returns:
1. Seeking confirmation of your beliefs (confirmation bias)
2. Mistaking recent events for ongoing trends (recency bias)
3. Overrating your own abilities (overconfidence bias)
4. Believing you're a worse investor than you are (attribution bias)
5. Trying to time the market (impossible without 69-91% accuracy)
6. Falling for the casino mentality (speculation vs. investing)

(Ch 6)

**Mutual Fund Saturation** — ~9,500 mutual funds in America—more than double publicly traded US companies. More funds = more opportunities to overcharge. (Ch 3)

**Negativity Bias** — Humans recall negative experiences more vividly than positive ones (evolutionary survival mechanism). In investing, causes fear-based selling during downturns. (Ch 2, 7)

**Pay-to-Play Corruption** — 401(k) providers accept payments from mutual fund companies to include funds on plan menu. Funds pay to play, not because they're best performers. Result: expensive actively managed funds dominate plan options. (Ch 4)

**90-Second Rule** — When you start suffering, give yourself 90 seconds to stop it and return to a beautiful state. Breathe, step back from stressful thoughts, focus on appreciation. "The best time to kill any monster is when it's little." (Ch 8)

**Principal-Agent Problem** — When advisors prioritize their compensation over clients' interests. Most "financial advisors" are brokers who earn commissions by selling products, not fiduciaries obligated to you. (Ch 5)

**Proprietary Products** — Many firms offer their own mutual funds or managed accounts. These generate additional revenue by steering you into highly profitable products for them (probably not for you). (Ch 5)

**Real Wealth** — Emotional, psychological, and spiritual wealth—not just financial. "If you're financially free, but you're still suffering emotionally, then what kind of victory is that?" Real wealth = financial + emotional richness. (Ch 9)

**Recency Bias** — Projecting recent trends into the future indefinitely. "Investors project out into the future what they have most recently been seeing" (Warren Buffett). (Ch 6)

**Recovery Problem** — Mathematical reality of losses: if you lose 50%, you need 100% gain to break even. Lose 75% and you need 300% gain. Makes loss avoidance critical. (Ch 6)

**Rebalancing** — Systematically selling assets that have grown and buying those that have declined. Forces you to buy low and sell high automatically. Set calendar dates or percentage thresholds (e.g., rebalance when asset allocation drifts 5%+ from target). (Ch 7)

**Registered Investment Advisor (RIA)** — Fiduciary who is legally required to act in clients' best interests. Independent RIAs have no broker-dealer affiliation. Ask 7 questions to verify. (Ch 5)

**Reticular Activating System** — Part of brain activated by desire/focus. Draws attention to whatever can help you achieve your goal. "Wherever your focus goes, your energy flows." (Ch 9)

**Revenue Sharing** — Legal but grubby practice in 401(k) plans: mutual funds pay providers to be included in plan menu. Equivalent to buying shelf space in store to sell crappy products. (Ch 4)

**Science of Achievement** — External world mastery. Rules of success you can follow (and be rewarded) or break (and be punished). Three steps: Focus → Massive Action → Grace. (Ch 9)

**Self-Directed Trap** — Some 401(k) providers allow self-directed accounts but charge additional 1.9% annually for "privilege" of managing your own money. "Heads you lose, tails I win." (Ch 4)

**Seven Key Questions to Ask Any Advisor** —
1. Are you a Registered Investment Advisor? (If no, run!)
2. Are you (or your firm) affiliated with a broker-dealer? (If yes, look for FINRA/SIPC and run!)
3. Does your firm offer proprietary mutual funds or managed accounts? (Want emphatic no)
4. Do you receive third-party compensation for recommending investments? (Want no—no kickbacks/commissions)
5. What's your investing philosophy? (Should not claim to beat market with stock picking)
6. What financial planning services beyond investing do you offer? (Tax, estate, college planning)
7. Where will my money be held? (Should use third-party custodian like Fidelity/Schwab—not the advisor)
(Ch 5)

**Stocks for the Long Run** — Jeremy Siegel's research showing stocks have outperformed bonds over any long-term period (20+ years) in US history. Despite volatility, equities build wealth over time. (Ch 2)

**Suffering State** — Emotional state of stress, worry, frustration, anger, depression, irritability, overwhelm, resentment, fear. "Stress" is just the achiever word for fear. Caused by undirected mind in survival mode. (Ch 8)

**Suitability Standard** — Brokers only need to meet "suitability" standard—products must be "suitable" for you but can still be expensive and underwhelming. Fiduciaries must act in your best interest. (Ch 5)

**Survival Software** — The human brain is a two-million-year-old organ designed for survival, not happiness. It constantly scans for threats, magnifies problems, and triggers fight-or-flight responses. Left unattended, creates chronic stress and suffering. (Ch 8)

**Swensen's Yale Model** — Asset allocation model that achieved 13.7% annual returns (1985-2015) with lower volatility: 30% US stocks, 20% foreign developed markets, 10% emerging markets, 20% REITs, 20% bonds. (Ch 7)

**Tax Efficiency** — Core Principle 3 of the Core Four. Focus on after-tax returns, not pre-tax. "It's not what you earn, it's what you keep." Use tax-advantaged accounts (401k, IRA, Roth IRA, HSA, 529 plans). (Ch 6)

**Tax-Loss Harvesting** — Selling investments at a loss to offset capital gains taxes. Can deduct up to $3,000 against ordinary income annually. Must watch 30-day wash-sale rule. Crashes create opportunities for this. (Ch 7)

**Tax Drag** — Taxes are often the largest expense in life. Active funds generate unnecessary tax bills through constant trading (short-term gains taxed at ordinary income rates). (Ch 3, 6)

**Third-Party Compensation** — The ultimate question: does your advisor receive kickbacks, commissions, consulting fees, trips, or other goodies for recommending specific investments? (Ch 5)

**Third-Party Custodian Protection** — Advisors should use independent custodians (Fidelity, Schwab, TD Ameritrade) to hold your funds. You sign limited power of attorney for management only—never withdrawals. This protects you from Bernie Madoff-style cons and lets you fire advisors without moving accounts. (Ch 5)

**Three Buckets** — Asset allocation framework:
- Bucket 1: Security bucket (0-5 years) — protects during downturns
- Bucket 2: Growth bucket (5+ years) — fuels long-term wealth
- Bucket 3: Dream bucket — life goals
(Ch 7)

**Three Suffering Triggers** — Patterns that account for most, if not all, suffering:
1. **Loss**: Focusing on what you've lost or will lose
2. **Less**: Believing you'll have less of what you value
3. **Never**: Convinced you'll never have something you value
(Ch 8)

**Three Steps to Achievement** —
1. **Focus**: "Wherever your focus goes, your energy flows." Activates reticular activating system.
2. **Massive Action**: Take massive action, change approach until it works. Model successful people.
3. **Grace**: Acknowledge luck/blessings. "The more you acknowledge grace, the more you seem to have it."
(Ch 9)

**Transaction Costs** — Commissions every time fund buys/sells stocks. Forbes estimated 1.44% annual cost for actively managed funds. (Ch 3)

**Two Principles of Fulfillment** —
1. **Keep Growing**: "Everything in life either grows or dies." Secret to happiness in one word: progress.
2. **You Have to Give**: "You make a living by what you get. You make a life by what you give." We're driven to contribute.
(Ch 9)

**Ultimate Decision** — "Are you committed to being happy, no matter what happens to you?" Will you enjoy life when everything goes your way AND when everything goes against you? This single decision can change everything. (Ch 8, 9)

**Unshakeable** — Unwavering and undisputed confidence; steadfast commitment to truth; presence, peace of mind, and calm amidst the storm. A state of mind, not just a matter of money. (Ch 1)

**Zero-Sum Trading** — When two people trade a stock, one must win and one must lose. Winner must win by big enough margin to cover transaction costs AND taxes. (Ch 3)
