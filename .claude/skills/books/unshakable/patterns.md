# Unshakeable Patterns & Techniques

## Core Four Implementation

### Don't Lose — Loss Avoidance Strategies
**When to use**: Portfolio construction, risk management, investment decision-making
**How**:
- Avoid investments that could permanently impair capital
- Understand mathematical reality: 50% loss requires 100% gain to recover
- Focus on capital preservation as priority #1
- Ask: "What's the downside risk?" before considering upside
- Quality survives downturns; speculation gets destroyed
**Trade-offs**: Conservative allocation may cap upside during bull markets but protects during crashes

### Asymmetric Risk/Reward — 5-to-1 Rule
**When to use**: Evaluating any investment opportunity
**How**: Paul Tudor Jones' five questions before any investment:
1. What's the hard trade? (uncomfortable but necessary)
2. What's asymmetric risk/reward? (disproportionate upside vs. downside)
3. Is it 5-to-1 or 3-to-1? (risk $1 to make $5 or $3)
4. What's the entry point? (target buy price)
5. Where are the stops? (exit if wrong)
**Trade-offs**: Fewer opportunities meet this criteria, but quality over quantity

### Tax Efficiency — Asset Location Strategy
**When to use**: Portfolio construction, asset placement across accounts
**How**:
- Place tax-inefficient assets in tax-advantaged accounts: bonds, REITs, high-dividend stocks
- Place tax-efficient assets in taxable accounts: index funds, ETFs, tax-managed funds
- Use tax-advantaged accounts: 401(k), IRA, Roth IRA, HSA, 529 plans
- Practice tax-loss harvesting during downturns (watch 30-day wash-sale rule)
- Focus on after-tax returns, not pre-tax
**Trade-offs**: May require coordination across multiple accounts, adds complexity

### Four-Level Diversification
**When to use**: Portfolio construction, risk management
**How**:
1. **Across asset classes**: Stocks, bonds, real estate, gold, commodities
2. **Within asset classes**: Don't bet on one stock or one sector
3. **Across markets/countries/currencies**: US + international + emerging markets
4. **Across time**: Dollar-cost averaging (invest regularly regardless of market conditions)
**Trade-offs**: Diversification reduces volatility but also caps upside during booms

## Asset Allocation Models

### Ray Dalio's All Weather Portfolio
**When to use**: Seeking stability in any economic environment (inflation, deflation, growth, recession)
**How**: 30% stocks, 40% long-term bonds, 15% intermediate-term bonds, 7.5% gold, 7.5% commodities
**Trade-offs**: Lower volatility, but may underperform pure stock portfolios in long bull markets

### David Swensen's Yale Model
**When to use**: Institutional investors or individuals seeking growth with lower volatility
**How**: 30% US stocks, 20% foreign developed markets, 10% emerging markets, 20% REITs, 20% bonds
**Trade-offs**: Requires rebalancing discipline; achieved 13.7% annual returns (1985-2015)

### Three Buckets Strategy
**When to use**: Life-stage planning, risk management, mental accounting
**How**:
- **Bucket 1** (Security): Cash, bonds for 0-5 years. Protects during downturns. Covers expenses when markets crash.
- **Bucket 2** (Growth): Stocks, real estate for 5+ years. Fuels long-term wealth. Time horizon allows riding out volatility.
- **Bucket 3** (Dream): Enjoyment, legacy funding. Life goals beyond necessities.
**Trade-offs**: Requires定期 rebalancing between buckets; may miss opportunities if allocations too rigid

## Systematic Rebalancing

### Calendar-Based Rebalancing
**When to use**: Portfolio maintenance, enforcing discipline
**How**: Set specific dates (e.g., quarterly, semi-annually, annually) to rebalance portfolio back to target allocations. Sell assets that have grown, buy assets that have declined.
**Trade-offs**: Simple and disciplined, but may trade too frequently (transaction costs) or not frequently enough (drift from targets)

### Threshold-Based Rebalancing
**When to use**: Portfolio maintenance, responsive rebalancing
**How**: Rebalance when asset allocation drifts by predetermined percentage (e.g., 5% or 10% from target). If stocks should be 60% but grow to 66%+, sell 6% and buy underweighted assets.
**Trade-offs**: More responsive to market moves but requires regular monitoring

## Bear Market Navigation

### Pre-Flight Preparation
**When to use**: Before market turbulence begins
**How**:
- Educate yourself about historical bear markets (frequency, duration, recovery)
- Write down why you own each asset class
- Document your investment plan in writing when calm and unemotional
- Understand how each asset class performed in previous downturns
- Know your advisor's plan for using turbulence to your advantage
**Trade-offs**: Time-consuming upfront, but prevents panic-selling during crashes

### In-Crisis Discipline
**When to use**: During market crashes and corrections
**How**:
- Review your written plan (why you own each asset)
- Remember: every bear market in US history has been followed by bull market
- Don't check portfolio daily (amplifies fear)
- Use dollar-cost averaging to buy more shares at lower prices
- Focus on long-term goals, not short-term fluctuations
- Consider tax-loss harvesting opportunities
**Trade-offs**: Requires emotional discipline; counterintuitive to buy when others are selling

### Howard Marks' Crisis Strategy
**When to use**: During severe market dislocations (2008-style crashes)
**How**: When "everybody is suicidal," that's a good time to buy. Oaktree Capital invested $500M/week for 15 weeks during late 2008 financial crisis. Made billions when markets recovered.
**Trade-offs**: Requires courage, cash reserves, and ability to withstand further declines

## Advisor Selection

### Seven-Question Vetting Process
**When to use**: Evaluating potential financial advisors
**How**: Ask these 7 questions—emphatic "no" desired for questions 3-4:
1. Are you a Registered Investment Advisor? (YES required)
2. Are you (or your firm) affiliated with a broker-dealer? (NO required)
3. Does your firm offer proprietary mutual funds or managed accounts? (NO required)
4. Do you receive third-party compensation for recommending investments? (NO required)
5. What's your investing philosophy? (Should NOT claim to beat market via stock picking)
6. What financial planning services beyond investing do you offer? (Tax, estate, college planning—comprehensive)
7. Where will my money be held? (Third-party custodian: Fidelity, Schwab, TD Ameritrade—NOT advisor)
**Trade-offs**: Fewer advisors pass this test, but quality over quantity

### Fiduciary Verification
**When to use**: Verifying advisor's legal status
**How**:
- Ask: "Are you a Registered Investment Advisor?"
- Look for "Securities offered through [company], member FINRA and SIPC" on business cards/websites
- If you see FINRA/SIPC, they're a broker—run!
- Verify via FINRA BrokerCheck or SEC Investment Advisor Public Disclosure (IAPD)
**Trade-offs**: Additional due diligence time, but prevents conflicts of interest

## Psychology Mastery

### 90-Second Rule (Stopping Suffering)
**When to use**: When you start feeling stressed, angry, fearful, or overwhelmed
**How**:
1. **Catch yourself** when tension rises in your body
2. **Gently breathe and slow down**—step out of the situation
3. **Step back from stressful thoughts**—don't believe them, just observe
4. **Find something to appreciate**—your breath, your heartbeat, being alive
**Trade-offs**: Takes practice to master (Tony started with "four-hour rule," now 90 seconds), but becomes automatic over time

### Gratitude Meditation (Two-Minute Alignment)
**When to use**: Shifting from suffering to beautiful state; daily practice recommended
**How**:
1. Pick a stressful issue (rank 6-7/10)
2. Place hands on heart, close eyes, breathe deeply into heart
3. Feel grateful for your heart (beats 100K times daily, 60K miles of blood vessels—gift you didn't earn)
4. Think of three experiences you're grateful for, step into each memory as if reliving it
5. Feel grateful for the gift of this moment
**Result**: Heart and brain rhythms synchronize (EEG and EKG lines become rounded and identical)—flow state
**Trade-offs**: Requires daily practice for maximum benefit

### Three Suffering Triggers Pattern Recognition
**When to use**: Identifying and stopping suffering before it escalates
**How**: Recognize when your mind is focusing on:
1. **Loss**: What you've lost or will lose (love, respect, money, opportunity)
2. **Less**: Believing you'll have less joy, money, success, or something you value
3. **Never**: Convinced you'll never have something you value (love, joy, respect, wealth, opportunity)
**Response**: Shift focus to appreciation ("What's right is always available... but so is what's wrong!")
**Trade-offs**: Takes ongoing awareness but prevents prolonged suffering

### Confirmation Bias Counteraction
**When to use**: Making investment decisions, evaluating investment ideas
**How**:
- Actively seek qualified opinions that differ from your own
- Ask: "Where could I be wrong? What am I not seeing? What's the downside?"
- Find people you respect who think differently
- Remember: Warren Buffett consults Charlie Munger to challenge his thinking
**Trade-offs**: Uncomfortable to hear contradictory views, but prevents costly mistakes

## Achievement & Fulfillment

### Three-Step Achievement Framework
**When to use**: Pursuing any significant goal (financial or otherwise)
**How**:
1. **Focus**: "Wherever your focus goes, your energy flows." Intense focus activates reticular activating system, drawing attention to resources that can help.
2. **Massive Action**: Take massive action, change approach until it works. Model successful people (Buffett, Dalio, Bogle, Swensen) to accelerate learning.
3. **Grace**: Acknowledge luck/blessings. "The more you acknowledge grace in your life, the more you seem to have it."
**Trade-offs**: Requires sustained effort and adaptability

### Two-Principle Fulfillment Framework
**When to use**: Creating sustainable happiness and life satisfaction
**How**:
1. **Keep Growing**: "Everything in life either grows or dies." Secret to happiness in one word: progress. Stagnation creates misery regardless of net worth.
2. **You Have to Give**: "You make a living by what you get. You make a life by what you give." We're driven to contribute. Contribution creates fulfillment.
**Trade-offs**: Requires ongoing effort but delivers lasting satisfaction

### The Ultimate Decision
**When to use**: Life transformation, commitment to happiness
**How**: Make this decision: "Are you committed to being happy, no matter what happens to you?" Will you enjoy life when everything goes your way AND when everything goes against you?
**Burn the boats**: 100% responsibility for your state of mind. Draw a line in the sand: "I'm done with suffering. I'm going to live every day to the fullest and find juice in every moment, including the ones I don't like, BECAUSE LIFE IS JUST TOO SHORT TO SUFFER."
**Trade-offs**: Requires unwavering commitment but delivers immediate freedom

## Crisis Preparation (Financial)

### Write Your Investment Rationale
**When to use**: Before market turbulence begins
**How**: Document why you own each asset class in your portfolio. Include:
- Expected role in portfolio (growth, stability, inflation hedge, etc.)
- Historical performance during different market conditions
- How long you're willing to hold if underperforms
**Use**: Review during turbulence to remind yourself why you invested and prevent panic-selling
**Trade-offs**: Time-consuming upfront, but prevents emotional decisions during crashes

### Advisor as Copilot
**When to use**: Navigating market turbulence
**How**: Have a financial advisor who can talk you through fears and concerns during difficult times, reminding you that the strategy you agreed upon in writing when calm and unemotional is still valid.
**Analogy**: "It's a bit like flying a plane through a really rough storm. Most pilots would be just fine if they were flying solo. But it's a whole lot easier when you know you've got an experienced copilot in the seat beside you! Remember: even Warren Buffett has a partner."
**Trade-offs**: Advisory fees, but value of emotional guidance during crashes often exceeds cost

## Notes

**Pattern Quality**: These patterns are drawn from 50+ financial masters interviewed in the book, including Warren Buffett, Ray Dalio, Jack Bogle, David Swensen, Paul Tudor Jones, Carl Icahn, and others.

**Implementation Priority**: Start with Core Four (Don't Lose, Asymmetric Risk/Reward, Tax Efficiency, Diversification) + Psychology Mastery (90-Second Rule, Gratitude Meditation). These provide highest return on investment with manageable effort.

**Customization Required**: Asset allocation models are starting points, not rigid prescriptions. Customize to your goals, time horizon, risk tolerance, and life circumstances.

**Systematic > Discretionary**: Wherever possible, choose systematic approaches (rebalancing rules, written plans, pre-determined allocations) over discretionary decisions. Systematic solutions remove emotion from investing.

**Psychology Is Primary**: 80% of investing success is psychology, 20% is mechanics. Master your mind first; mechanics follow naturally.
